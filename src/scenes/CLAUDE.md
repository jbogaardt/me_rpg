# src/scenes (the overworld conductor, zones and doors)

The Dungeon Master's controls (bar, director, cards, DM quests) are in `docs/dungeon-master.md`; spells and combos in `docs/systems/magic.md`. Detail moved out of the root `CLAUDE.md` (which has the layers, commands, procedure and conventions); words such as "above" or "below" inside these bullets refer to this file or the map in the root. Keep the bullet for a feature up to date in the same change.

- **`src/scenes/OverworldScene.ts` and `src/scenes/overworld/`** — the overworld is a world of zones (`ZoneState.ts`: one loaded `MapData` with its mobs, chests, shopkeepers, ground items and effects). `OverworldScene` is only the conductor (about 380 lines): the frame loop, the HTML overlays (menu, dialog, shop counter, trade window, map) and what the action key does on the spot (talk to the NPC or a shopkeeper, open a chest, pick up, otherwise attack). **Put new code in the file that owns the concern, not in the scene**: `Arena.ts` (who is in the game: `local` is the player at this keyboard and `remotes` the friends by player number, player 0 being the host; the loaded zones; `role`, the clock, the `Weather`; and the **zone context**: the rules are written once and serve every zone, `arena.zone` is the zone being worked in and a per-zone loop wraps its work in `arena.inZone(z, ...)`, so `arena.mobs`, `.collision`, `.effects`, `.chests`, `.ground`, `.players()` answer for that zone), `Zones.ts` (loading zones, prefetching those behind doors, `travel` through a door with the iris `ZoneTransition`, showing the local player's zone on the stage, and a guest following the host's zone), `Simulation.ts` (the rules, solo and host only: moving incl. ice, cooldowns and regeneration, sword/bow/wand and tome (`Casting.ts`), mob shots, damage from mobs and lightning, XP and gold, defeat; what a friend should see goes through its `emit`), `Visuals.ts` (draws slashes, bolts, arrows, numbers, particles, poses and lightning; the simulation and a guest both use it, `show(event)` is how a guest draws what the host reports), `Ground.ts` (items on the floor: drop, pick up, crumble, and a guest's synced list), `PackSync.ts` (gold, pack and gear: the local player's Inventory actions and trades, the host doing a friend's requests and sending packs, a guest applying them), `Trading.ts` (player-to-player trades: the host's `TradeBook`, a guest's command queue, the trade screens), `HostLink.ts` and `GuestLink.ts` (the two sides of the snapshot/input protocol, below) and `constants.ts` (shared tuning). A new rule belongs in `Simulation`, a new thing to draw in `Visuals`, a new thing that is synced in co-op in the matching link. Each frame the scene reads `Session.role` (`solo`/`host`/`client`, see Co-op); combat is per `Player` (`entities/Player.ts`: body + hero + cooldowns), driven by a `Command` from the keyboard or the network. Solo, a dialog freezes the world and the menu pauses it. New areas are new zones (or new scenes), not more code in the scene.
- **Zones and doors (several maps, one world)** — a *zone* is one `MapData`: the ten open-air biomes of the world (`greenwood`, the start, `town`, `eastwood`, `desert`, `beach`, `undead`, `jungle`, `wetlands`, `marsh`, `frostlands`; see Map pipeline → World) and a room per shop (`shop_smithy`, `shop_armory`, `shop_apothecary`, `shop_ranger`, `shop_wizard`). `MapData.doors` (`z.door(col, row, w, toZone, toCol, toRow)`) are cells that take a player to another zone; `MapData.indoor` (`z.indoor()`) rooms get no weather, ambient or overview map. Pixi-free geometry in `map/doors.ts` (`doorAt`, `doorsNear`, `arrivalFacing`, tested). `scenes/ZoneState.ts` bundles everything per loaded zone (`MapView`, `Collision`, `Camera`, `Effects`, mobs, chests, shopkeepers, ground items, `heroBodies`); `OverworldScene` keeps `zones` (by name) and each `Player` has a `zone`. **The scene works in one zone at a time through `this.zone`**: outside the per-zone loops it is the local player's zone (`this.view`, the only one drawn, swapped onto `stage` by `showZone`, under a `ZoneTransition`), and `inZone(z, fn)` points it at another zone for a moment so one set of rules (`mobs`, `chests`, `players()`, `bodies()`...) serves all of them; `players()` is the players in the current zone, `everyone()` all of them. **Efficiency**: only zones with a player in them are simulated (`occupiedZones`), so a shop nobody is in, or a zone everyone has left, costs nothing; zones load on demand (`ensureZone` via `assets.loadZone`) and `prefetchDoors` starts loading the zone behind any door within 5 cells of a player (host: of every player; guest: of the local one) so stepping through is instant, and a door whose zone is not ready yet simply waits a frame. **Memory stays bounded**: `Zones.retire` unloads a zone nobody has been in or near a door into for `RETIRE_AFTER` (20 s) (never the start zone, which a defeat wakes you in): `ZoneState.dispose` frees its map, mobs and characters (their sheets go back to the wardrobe), `assets.unloadZone` frees its map and every tileset no other loaded zone uses (tilesets are reference-counted), and what must last is kept in `Arena.memory` (`ZoneMemory`: opened chests and the items on the ground, with their ages; `Ground.stash`/`restore` include it) and given back by `Zones.add` when it loads again (its mobs start afresh). A guest counts only itself. A zone that is loaded but not shown has its map sprites freed (`MapView.release`). **Travel** (`travel`): host/solo move the player (`doorAt` each frame, not while reading a dialog/shop), set their zone, place them at the door's `to` cell facing away from the door, give `ARRIVE_GRACE` immunity (`Player.grace`, 1.8 s, longer than the transition because the world runs behind it; silent, unlike the blinking `invuln` after a hit, and `Player.safe` is either) and play a **door transition** for the local player (`beginTrip`): **walking from one open-air zone into the next is not a transition at all** (`Zones.seam`: both zones open-air and the door names its direction, i.e. a gap in the border). The zones are laid out like one map: `neighbourLinks` (`map/doors.ts`, Pixi-free) gives, for each gap, where the other zone's coordinates sit against this one (`to - door - direction * (1 + ARRIVE_CELLS)` cells; `tools/map/links.test.ts` checks it against the built maps), and while a player is in a zone the open-air zones joined to it are drawn beside it, nested in the player's own world at that offset (`Zones.syncNeighbours`/`update`: they share its camera, `MapView.update` builds their own view rect) and layered between its ground and its objects, so every zone's ground is under everyone's trees (a crown never sinks into the next zone's grass); a zone to the south (`Link.dir` `down`) goes over the player's objects, any other under, each drawing only its own cells (`MapView` skips the neighbour margin, surface `beyond`, which the neighbour draws), so the two join without a seam and you see the next biome's land, props, NPCs and chests before you reach it. Crossing a gap only changes which map the player is in: the new zone's camera `adopt`s the exact screen position the old one had (nothing moves on screen), the old zone stays drawn beside it, the hero's drawing (`Character.pivot`) eases up to their new place (the arrival cell is `1 + ARRIVE_CELLS` ahead of the door), the last zone's weather fades out over `WEATHER_FADE` as the new one's fades in, and `AreaBanner` names the new area. A zone seen from the next one over is a lookout (`ZoneState.setLookout`): its weather is hidden, and on a solo game or a host its mobs are drawn and kept wandering (the scene updates them with no one to chase) so nothing looks frozen or pops in as you cross; a guest, who is not told what they are doing, hides them. `Zones.prefetch` loads the zones joined by a gap when a player is within `BORDER_REACH` (30 cells) of the neighbours' margin (`nearBorder`), and `retire` keeps them (and every zone shown) loaded. A guest does the same on `follow`. Everything else (a shop or cave door, a trial, waking after a defeat) is the **iris**, one look: `map/transition.ts` (Pixi-free, tested). A black screen with a round hole closes on the hero in the old view (a still picture of the stage taken just before the swap), stays black a beat (or as long as a zone still loading needs), then opens round the hero in the live new zone, which settles in from a 12% closer shot (`Camera.punch`, `settleAt`) while the rim of the circle glows in the colour of the zone being entered (`zoneTint` in `systems/places.ts`, `rimAlpha`); about 0.9 s. It is drawn as a thick stroke rather than a rectangle with a hole cut in it: a `cut()` hole that crosses the rectangle's edge, as a circle larger than the screen does, draws solid black and flashes the screen. Do not add another look: a new door either joins two open-air zones by a gap (seamless) or uses the iris. `entities/ZoneTransition.ts` draws it over the stage (the scene's `root` is `top` = stage + transition); the local player stands still and takes no door while it plays. Purely visual: the simulation moved the player at once, so it is the same for solo, host and guest. **Co-op**: players can be in different zones at once. The host simulates every occupied zone and sends each friend a snapshot of *their* zone only (`SnapMsg.zone`, plus `away`: the slots of players in other zones, so they are not mistaken for having left; mobs, chests and events are that zone's; friends in the same zone share one built snapshot per tick). A guest that finds itself in a new zone (`followToZone`) loads it if needed (the iris closes on the old zone and holds shut meanwhile), plays the same transition, moves its own body there and re-places the others from the list. Events carry their zone (`emit`). `tools/map/check.ts` `checkDoors` (run by `npm run map -- check`) fails a door to a missing zone, one arriving on a wall or another door, or with no door back. Interiors are built by `tools/map/interiors.ts` (`shopInterior`) from `interior.png` (floor, wall, doormat, counter, shelves, signs), drawn by `tools/map/interior.ts` (part of `npm run tilesets`) in the shop buildings' palette; the town's door cells are `SHOP_DOORS` in `maps/town.ts` (world cells), which the shop rooms read through `shopExit` (in the town's own cells). `checkDoors` runs over the biomes too. **`checkCrossings` (also in `npm run map -- check`, with `tools/map/walk.ts`) walks the real collision box through every directional door: the arrival fits, the way ahead stays open for 6 cells (no bush in the doorway) and a body can walk 30 cells on (or reach the spawn), so no crossing drops a player into a pocket.**
