# towercraft: a small real-time strategy game on a single road

A separate game on the same site (built into `dist/towercraft/`, see the root `CLAUDE.md`). It shares no code with Me RPG; only
the conventions (Vite, strict TypeScript, Pixi-free rules with unit tests beside them, Prettier, ESLint).

## The game

- **One road, two castles, five maps.** One long winding road (36x55 cells, 175-225 long, units walk 2-6 cells a second) joins player 0's castle to player 1's; units meet in between. The player picks the map on the title screen, above the race (kept in `localStorage` as `towercraft:map`): `greenwood` (the classic straight switchbacks, the sim's default), `frostlands` (the menu's default), `desert`, `mire` (swamp) and `ember` (zigzags between lava), each drawn in the look of the Me RPG biome of that name. The map lives in `Match.board` (`Board` in `board.ts`: road, castles, `roadCells`, `sceneryCells`, `pointAt`, `headingAt`), so the rules never read a map constant. You win by destroying the other castle (or, after `TIME_LIMIT`, by castle health left).
- **The dock and the card** (Me RPG's menu kit: gold frames over wood and parchment, Cinzel and Crimson; `index.html`): the bottom dock is small thumbnails to train, build and command (price under each, `L2` if the castle is too low); tap one and a *card* pops up at the side (above the dock on a narrow screen) with its stats and the purchase button (Train; Build, which arms placement; Upgrade for the castle card, also opened by tapping the castle).
- **Orders** (`Orders`, `setOrder`): a player's units either stop and fight the towers and units they meet (`attack`) or walk on past (`bypass`), chosen separately; the castle is always attacked once in reach.
- **Two currencies, wood and stone** (`Cost`, `price()` in `races.ts`; `Player.wood/stone`): every unit, building, upgrade and castle level costs both. The castle pays `CastleDef.income[level]` every `PAY_EVERY` (5 s); mills (wood) and quarries (stone) (`TowerDef.econ`, `limit` 3 each, a different picture per race) add more, and razing one cuts the foe's income (`incomeOf`). **A purchase is instant**: no queue, no train time, no cooldown on spamming (`trainUnit`); only the money and `UNIT_CAP` (120 a side) limit it.
- **The castle sells only its base unit; producer buildings unlock the rest** (`UnitDef.needs` = a building id, `TowerDef.trains` lists what it unlocks, `unitUnlocked`/`towerUnlocked`): the Goblin Hatchery, Den, Bat Aerie and Dread Roost, the Dwarf Hall, Yale Pen, Eyrie and Sky Forge, the Germanic Barracks, Stables, Workshop, Falconry and Griffin Roost. A building may need another (`TowerDef.needs`, a small tech tree) and may be limited (`limit`). Razing a producer locks its units again (units already out stay). The units walk out from the castle and march on their own.
- **Attack styles** (`UnitDef`): `range` (a long-range unit hits across the gap to another run of the road), `splash` (a blast or a trample round the target; also hurts towers when the attacker may fight towers), `multi` (one blow strikes that many foes in range at once: a sweep or a flame), `suicide` (blows itself up on its first strike; the foe gets no bounty), `siege` (damage multiplier against towers and castles), `hitsAir`, `cloak`. Flyers (`air`) now **follow the road** like walkers, over everything; only air-capable things hurt them.
- **Units leave the road to take a tower** (`detourFor`, `Board.footOf`, `Unit.off`): a unit that cannot reach a tower from the road steps off it at the road point straight across from the tower (square to the road there, never a slant, at most `DETOUR_MAX` cells out) until it can hit it, fights from there, and returns to the road when the tower is down or the order becomes `bypass`.
- **Defence**: attackers (ground, air or `both`), anti-air-only towers, **bunkers** (`TowerDef.bunker`: `slots` and `rangeBonus`; `loadBunker` buys ONE soldier of a garrison-capable unit (`UnitDef.garrison`, the ranged ones) into a slot for its share of the price (`soldierCost`); each loaded soldier shoots from the bunker on its own cooldown with its own damage, range+bonus, `hitsAir`, upgrades; they die with the bunker), auras and detectors. Only the castle must fall; razing other buildings weakens the foe (income, units, upgrades, build reach).
- **Upgrades** (`UpgradeDef`, `RACES[race].upgrades`): per race four lines, ground damage / ground health / air damage / air health, three levels of +15% each (`boostOf`), bought at a smithy-type building (`needs`) for wood and stone once the castle level allows (`buyUpgrade`); they are instant too. Health applies to units bought afterwards (`Unit.maxHp`), damage to all of them at once.
- **Towers defend**: they shoot enemy units only. Units attack units, towers and castles.
- **Tower roles** (`TowerDef`; `roleOf` in `ai.ts`): *attackers* hit `ground`, `air` or `both`, maybe with `splash` or poison (`dot`, renews, never stacks); *auras* `power` (+% damage, at most double) or `heal` friends and fire nothing; *detectors* (`detect`) reveal cloaked enemies; *bunkers*, *producers*, *economy* buildings and *smithies* as above. `damage: 0` never shoots.
- **Unit traits**: `air` units follow the road and only air-capable things (`hits` air/both towers, `hitsAir` units, bunkered soldiers that have it, the castle) hurt them; `cloak` units are untargetable unless `visibleTo` finds a detector (or the castle's sight) near.
- **Build reach**: towers go on free cells inside the castle's reach (6, 9, 13 by level) or a built tower's `reach`, so a front line is pushed forward tower by tower. Castle upgrades unlock tier 2-3 units and towers, pay and health.
- **Fog of war and sight** (`visionOf`, `canSee`, `isSeen`, `isCastleSeen`): castles, towers and units have a `sight` radius; a player sees those circles plus the ground they may build on. The build area is permanent revealed ground and the only place to build; tower and castle sight last while they stand; **unit sight is temporary** and never extends the build area. Enemies outside every circle are hidden: not drawn, targeted or splashed; cloaked ones need sight *and* a detector. The AI reads the foe through vision too. `main.ts` draws the fog like Me RPG's underground darkness (a tiny canvas scaled up smooth). Placing shows a ghost with range, reach and sight rings; the hint line says why a cell is refused.
- **Everything that fights is drawn at twice its old size** (`SIZE` in `main.ts`) so it stands in proportion to the roads; roads are three cells wide (`halfWidth`).
- **Animation** (`main.ts`, all cosmetic, read from the sim's `Shot.by`): a unit walks (frames follow the distance walked, five directions along the road's tangent), stands (breathing), flaps if it flies, and for `STRIKE_TIME` after a blow faces its target and plays its attack frames with a lunge (melee) or recoil (shot); missiles fly. A tower breathes, kicks back and flares a lamp when it fires; healers and power towers breathe a halo, detectors sweep (`makeTowerFx`, `animateTower`). Keep it quiet: no sparkles.
- **Races** differ by how their numbers trade off (`races.ts`): swarm/Goblins (fast, weak, bought in groups, suicide boomers and bomb bats, cloaked thieves), titan/Dwarves
  (slow, strong, costly: thunderers, flame runesmiths, thanes, yale riders, gryphons; long-range towers, tough bunker), legion/Germanic (the yardstick: archers, saxons that sweep, powder sappers, field catapults that outrange towers, ritters, falcons and griffins). Each has 4-6 ground units and 2-3 air units. A new race is an entry in `races.ts`; `races` tests check its tech tree is closed (every `needs`/`trains`/upgrade building exists).

## Code

- **Rules are Pixi-free and deterministic** (`src/sim/`, an ESLint rule enforces it): `sim.ts` is the whole match, `races.ts` the data,
  `board.ts` the road and castles (a `Board`; a match carries its own, `Match.board`, and the rules read that, never a constant: the game plays `DEFAULT_BOARD`), `ai.ts` the opponent (seeded, so a match replays). `main.ts` draws a `Match`, owns the camera (drag to
  pan, wheel or pinch to zoom, so a phone can place towers precisely) and turns taps into commands. `?debug` exposes the match to playtest scripts. The same rules can later run on a server.
- **Art** is Wyrmsun's, copied by `tools/towercraftArt.ts` from a clone of its repo (`public/art/CREDITS.md`): animated unit sheets
  (CC-BY-SA 3.0, so the title screen credits them and the terms follow the art), CC0 castles, towers and portraits. The fields (`art/maps/<id>.png`, adaptations of the LPC terrain, so also CC-BY-SA) are drawn by Me RPG's
  map pipeline with `npm run towercraft:board [-- <id>]` (`tools/towercraftBoard.ts`: one `Theme` per map, the biome's ground, trees, bushes and rocks), which also writes `src/sim/scenery.ts` (the cells woods, lakes and rocks cover on each map: nothing is built there); the roads are `src/sim/road.ts` (`ROADS`: a straight-run list, or control points for player 0's half that `meander` smooths and turns), then re-run it. **A new map** is a `ROADS` entry, a `THEMES` entry and a run of the generator; `board.test.ts` checks it.
  Only the classic `greenwood` road is turned half way round onto itself; the others wander freely (not symmetric) and `board.test.ts` keeps them fair: both castles must have about the same free ground in reach (keep castles off the field's edges), and balance is judged with sides alternating. The UI kit is Me RPG's (`public/ui/`, copied from `src/ui/`).
- Headless screenshots: `--use-angle=swiftshader`.

## State of play and the plan (StarCraft-style depth, in slices)

Done: five maps, castles, upgrades, combat, towers, fog, orders, card UI, animation, balance; the wood/stone economy, producer buildings, bunkers, unit upgrades, attack styles (ranged, suicide, multi-hit, splash), flyers on the road and units that leave it for towers.

**The lab** (`npm run towercraft:lab -- --help`, `tools/towercraftLab.ts`; not part of the game): headless AI-against-AI matches over maps (`maps.ts`: symmetric balance
maps, the game's maps are `BOARDS`) x races x strategies (`STRATEGIES` in `ai.ts`), with win rates and 95% intervals, a CSV (`--out`) and what-if overrides on the
race data (`--set swarm.units.glider.hp=*1.2`). Sim constants are edited in source.

**Balance** is measured with the lab or `npm run towercraft:balance [-- N [seedBase]]` (`tools/towercraftBalance.ts`): re-run after changing `races.ts`, `ai.ts` or `sim.ts`
constants. `balance.test.ts` is a guard only. Last measured on greenwood after the wood/stone expansion (N 40, 240 matches): 95% decisive, median 8.2 min, Goblins 54%, Dwarves 46%, Germanic 50%. Levers: income (mills +6 wood, quarries +8 stone, castle income), prices (Dwarf x1.1, Goblin x0.9 of the first draft), hp, count, speed, splash. The economy needs both currencies: when stone was scarce (5 a quarry) wood piled up and the Dwarves turtled to a stalemate. **Miss:** Dwarf mirrors still time out about a third of the time (heavy towers and bunkers; the AI keeps at most `MAX_TOWERS` = 6 defensive towers, which fixed most of it); the first draft's numbers swung 30 points on a 10% price change, so judge by N of 100.

**The AI** (`ai.ts`; a `Style` drawn from its seed, since two equal armies cancel): builds **mills and quarries** first (`ecoEvery`) on cells near its castle, **tech** (producers and the smithy, `techStart`/`techEvery`), buys units in **waves** (at least `MIN_WAVE` units, never a trickle; bombs against towers, sweeps against crowds, archers against air), **buys upgrades** from spare money, builds **bunkers** and loads them, **upgrades the castle** on a schedule, adds towers **as far forward as they still see road**, builds a **detector**/**shooter** once the foe fields cloaked/air, walks **past towers** when its army could kill the castle in seconds, **past units** when it would win that race, and always after its `rush` time. It reads the foe only through `foeUnits`, `foeTowers` and `foeCastle` (vision).

Next: a *beacon* tower; **detector units**; **more roles** (slow, armour); **unit upgrades per type** (today they are per class); **control** (rally, attack-move, online); mines to take and hold on the road; tune the Dwarf mirror.
