# tools/map and maps/ (the map pipeline)

Also use the `build-zone` skill for any map change. Keep it up to date in the same change ("above"/"below" in the text mean this file or the root map).

- **Shop buildings** — `public/tilesets/town.png`, built by `tools/map/town.ts` (part of `npm run tilesets`) from the CC0 "Town RPG Pack" by Luis Zuno in `art/town_rpg_pack/` (16px art, doubled to the 32px grid): the pack's inn and tall house are cut out, roofs recoloured, the tall house given the inn's door, and an emblem painted over each door. Coordinates are `BUILDINGS` in `tools/map/catalog.ts`; the prefabs `smithy`, `apothecary`, `wizard` (6x7 cells) and `armory`, `ranger` (3x9) are solid from the ground up. Each building has a door (see Zones and doors) into its shop room.
- **Map pipeline** — maps are built by agents from code, not painted: **use the `build-zone` skill** (`.claude/skills/build-zone/SKILL.md`) for any map change.
  - `maps/<zone>.ts` (source of truth) uses the `Zone` builder (`tools/map/zone.ts`: `fill`, `patch`, `forest`, `prop`, `stamp`, `plateau`, `block`/`unblock`, `spawn`/`npc`/`mob`) with named assets from `tools/map/catalog.ts`. `npm run map -- build` compiles it to `public/maps/<zone>.json`, renders `maps/out/<zone>.png` + `.debug.png` (gitignored), and runs `tools/map/check.ts`. Never hand-edit the JSON; `npm run build` fails if it's stale or a check errors.
  - The JSON is a packed `MapData` (`PackedMap` v3 in `src/map/types.ts`, written by `tools/map/encode.ts`: ground cells index a palette of shared layer stacks, objects are `[part, px, py]` triples into a parts table; `parseMap` unpacks it, and still reads the older v2). `MapData`: 32px ground cells referencing 32x32 regions of tileset sheets (a cell may stack several `[sheet, x, y]` layers, bottom first: terrain edges drawn over the ground; `MapView` and the previews draw them in order), free-placed objects (y-sorted by bottom edge, or by an explicit `z` for flat art like cliffs; each can carry a `tag` such as tree/bush/rock/cliff), a collision cell list, per-cell terrain metadata (`surface` names like grass/sand/snow, `level` height, `ramps` = stairs) for game systems, spawn/NPC cells and mob spawns. The game reads collision, surface (footprints) and tags (leaf fall); `level`/`ramps` are for height-aware movement (see Roadmap). `MapView` renders it in the game; `tools/map/image.ts` renders the same way in Node for previews. Tilesets live in `public/tilesets/`; all art is cut on the 32px grid.
  - **Cliffs**: `z.plateau(rect, {kind, stairs})` builds a raised plateau from the Avalon blob on `Cliff_tileset.png` (cut along its grass hole so it scales to any size >= 4x4; measurements in `CLIFF`, `tools/map/catalog.ts`). `rect` is the walkable top; the rim ring and two face rows around it block; stairs are the only way up and become `ramps`. `kind` picks `grass`/`frost`/`dry` (`frost_cliffs.png`, `dry_cliffs.png`, derived in `recolor.ts`). The top is always the ground its rim is drawn with (`TOP_GROUND` in `zone.ts`: grass, snow, sand): `plateau` fills it and no terrain shows on it, painted before or after (a mesa in the desert's red dirt used to show a red middle inside a sand rim). The art is flat ground-level (`z` below all entities), so the hero draws over it. `check.ts` fails if two open cells of different level touch without stairs (a gap in a rim). `Extra_Unfinished4.png` (stone wall, door, brown drop-off) and `graphics-tiles-waterflow.png` (animated water) are still uncatalogued.
  - Collision for props is derived from their art (cells covered by the bottom `solid.band` px of the sprite), forests block their rect, so collision tracks the visuals. The tilesets have no foliage edge tiles — all vegetation is prefabs, and trees are cut from the LPC trees pack (see Trees and bushes).
  - **Terrain (autotiled ground)**: `z.paint(terrain, rects)` paints a terrain on cell *corners* from the LPC terrain atlas (`public/tilesets/lpc_terrain.png`, "Tiled Terrains" from OpenGameArt, source + Tiled tileset in `art/lpc_terrain/`; `tools/map/terrain.ts` reads which tile has which corners from `Terrain.tsx`), so any shape blends with soft edges and inner corners (dirt roads, sand, mud, ash, swamp water, ponds, ice, lava, pits...). Terrains stack over the cell's `fill` in `TERRAINS` order; the opaque `coast`/`snowCoast` bring their own sea. The atlas also gives full-cell grounds (`sea`, `cobble`, `flagstone`, `crackedStone`) and props (sandstone/basalt boulders, a standing stone, reeds, lily pads). `blob`, `pond`, `trail` and `reedsAt` in `tools/map/helpers.ts` make organic patches, ponds, winding trails and bank-side reeds; `tools/map/noise.ts` has the noise, `smoothMask` and `distanceField` that shape pools and ground edges. **`murk`** is swamp water: the atlas's soft-banked pond recoloured olive (`lpc_swamp.png`, `SWAMP_SHEET`, made by `recolor.ts`; a terrain may name its own `sheet`). The atlas's `Sewer Water` tile is rectilinear (a dungeon sewer) and is not used for swamps. **Reeds** (`reedBed`, `reedStalks`) are bank plants: put them on the land beside water, never in it (the atlas draws a water-edge strip under them, which is not used). The marsh keeps them two cells clear of its causeways, and their two foot rows block like a trunk's.
  - **Trees and bushes** come from the LPC trees pack (`art/lpc_trees/`, CC-BY-SA, see CREDITS.md), not the Avalon sheet. `tools/map/treePicks.ts` lists the picked sprites (rects that enclose one sprite), `tools/map/trees.ts` (part of `npm run tilesets`) cuts each out by its shape (keeping its translucent baked shadow), packs them into `public/tilesets/trees_<green|dry|jungle|dead|frost|swamp>.png` (frost and swamp recolour the green sheet) and writes `tools/map/treeAtlas.ts` (generated, never hand-edit). Every kind has six trees `treeA`..`treeF` (`frostTreeA`.., `dryTree..`, `jungleTree..`, `deadTree..`, `swampTree..`) and two bushes (`bush`, `bushSmall`; `frostBush`...); `TREES`, `FROST_TREES`, `DRY_TREES`, `JUNGLE_TREES`, `DEAD_TREES`, `SWAMP_TREES` list them for groves, walls and `scatter`. Collision: the bottom 75% of a sprite (`SOLID_SHARE`), the crown above it is walk-behind; `isArt` ignores translucent pixels so shadows never block. The leaf-fall (`entities/ambient.ts`) keys off the `tree`/`bush` tag and the sheet name (`trees_frost.png` → frost leaves), so a new kind needs a `PALETTES` key in its file name. To add or swap a tree, edit `treePicks.ts`, run `npm run tilesets`, then `npm run map -- build`.
  - **Recoloured sheets**: the Avalon pack only has grass/sand/water/stone, so snow and ice are *derived* sheets (`frost_ground.png`, `frost_objects.png` (blue-grey rocks), `ground_plus.png`) generated by `tools/map/recolor.ts` (`npm run tilesets`, outputs committed). Same coordinates as the originals, so catalog entries just point at another sheet; each has its own baked shadow colour (`SHADOWS`).
  - **World (the biomes)**: `maps/world.ts` lays the open-air world out after the hand-drawn map, one character per 10x10-cell square (34 x 23 squares = 340 x 230 cells), and lists the **gaps** between biomes. The borders don't follow the squares: `carve` (tools/map/world.ts) pushes each cell's lookup about with smooth noise (`wobble`, default 0.7 squares; `seed`), smooths away spikes and gives stray islands to their neighbour, so borders look carved, but runs them straight across every gap. Each biome is a `maps/<name>.ts` that default-exports `defineBiome({ name, climate, walls: { trees, fillers }, worldEdge?, build })` (`climate`: its weather, see Weather) and builds in *world* cells, placed in squares through `units(square)` (`at`, `area`) so the content scales with the layout. `buildWorld` (run by `npm run map`) walls every biome in along every border and the world's edge with `WALL` (3, a round band) cells of its own `woods`, cuts a 3-wide corridor through both walls at each gap (and keeps the way just past it open) with a door on each side leading straight across (`dir` set, arriving `ARRIVE` cells in), runs every spec, composes them into one world (`Zone.compose`: each cell from its owner; a see-through terrain fades across a border) and cuts each biome's map from it: its own cells plus `MARGIN` (14) cells of its neighbours, blocked (surface `beyond`); the game does not draw them in this zone's view but lays the neighbour's own map there (see Zones and doors), so the wall seen across a border *is* the neighbour's wall and the margin tells the game how the two maps line up. Pockets of open ground nobody can reach from the spawn are sealed. **Edges are never straight**: a wall's inner edge swells by up to `RAGGED` cells with noise on top of its `WALL` core (`World.wall`, from `World.borderDistance`, the distance of each cell to the nearest other biome); `z.ground(terrain)` lays a biome's ground terrain over its own cells but stops `inset` cells short of the borders (default 3 to 7, wandering), so the grounds on either side of a border never meet in a straight line (use it instead of `z.paint(terrain, z.bounds)`); pass `inset: [1, 2]` so the edge lies under the walls rather than leaving a ring of grass inside them (the Barrow Lands' ash). **Jarring ground fails the build**: `tools/map/ground.ts` (`groundReport`, run by `checkMap` on every open-air map, tested in `check.test.ts`) measures the floor as it is drawn (ground layers plus cliff art, Lab colour): a *hard seam* is a colour jump right on a cell boundary (`SEAM_STEP`; autotiled edges change colour inside the tile, so they pass; a fill against a fill, a paved rect, a plateau top unlike its rim, the sea against grass do not), and a *clash* is one surface in two shades further apart than `CLASH_DE` within two cells (a deep green over a pale grass). Seams under props, the edge of cliff art on open ground and the drawn banks of ponds, bogs, lava, pits and ice are exempt. Paved spots use `z.patch('stone', rect)` (its own grassy edge), not a `fill` blob; the desert and beach lay soft `sand` terrain over their base so they fade across borders; `redDirt` is its own surface (`clay`). **A biome's ground is a palette of at most two**: two grounds may share a biome if their colours are close and the edge between them is soft (an autotiled terrain edge, a shape from `blob`/`z.ground`, never a bare rect or a cell-stepped line). The LPC `meadow`/`darkGrass` are too far from the Avalon `grass` fill to pair with it, so the green biomes use `grass` alone for now (the cliff art's grass, so plateaus match: a plateau's top must be the ground it stands on); a palette-matched second grass would be a terrain recoloured to sit beside `grass` (`recolor.ts`, as `murk` is); a `grove` never grows onto a road (cells kept clear, plus one of verge) and `scatter` keeps a tall prop's canopy rows off a road too. Maps include `MARGIN` (22) cells of the neighbours, more than half of a 1280 px wide view. **`checkSeams` (tools/map/check.ts, run by `npm run map -- check` and the zone test) enforces that borders match**: along every border both sides are blocked, or both are doors straight across to each other, and nothing is open on the world's edge. The beach sets `worldEdge: false` and closes the north with the sea. **Woods** (`z.grove(area)`, and the walls) are any shape: trees standing wholly inside it on a staggered lattice, then fillers for the corners, and its cells block only where that art covers them (`SOFT_COVER`; the row along a border always), so the edge of a wood is as walkable as it looks. **No invisible walls**: a wall plants 55% of its lattice (`WALL_DENSITY`), but every cell of its border row gets a tree, else a bush or rock (`woods`), so each side of every border shows its own line of trees; a border cell nothing fits over is marked `BARE_WALL` and fails `checkMap`. Shapes come from `blob` (a lobed outline), trails from `trail` (winding between nodes, straight into a gap's corridor `worldOf(layout).gap(a, b)`), points from `z.near(col, row)` (the nearest free cell, whatever the border did). A biome spec also gets `bounds`, `owns`, `keepClear` and a deterministic `scatter`. Plan against `npm run map -- layout <biome>`; keep a gap away from a bend of its border. Snow and sand both blend into grass.
